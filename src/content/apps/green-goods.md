Green Goods is a local-first platform for documenting, verifying, and financing regenerative work. It helps communities record field activity, review and validate submissions, turn approved work into structured impact data, and connect that data to longer-term funding systems such as community treasuries, impact assets, and yield-backed capital formation.

The platform is designed for real-world coordination environments where work happens in neighborhoods, farms, watersheds, and local community networks rather than purely online spaces. Its offline-first workflow reduces dependence on continuous connectivity and lowers the operational barrier for contributors documenting work on the ground.

## What This App Does

Green Goods combines impact capture, verification, reporting, and funding coordination in one system. In practice, it enables communities to:

- document ecological and community work through local-first tools
- review and verify submissions using operator and evaluator workflows
- create structured records that support reporting and accountability
- aggregate approved work into reusable impact outputs, including attestations and impact assets
- connect verified work to treasury growth and longer-term regenerative financing models

## Core Functionality

### Offline-First Impact Capture

Contributors can document work using field-oriented workflows that support photos, notes, metadata, and activity records without assuming reliable connectivity at the point of capture. This makes the system more practical for real-world community work than tools designed primarily for desktop-first coordination.

### Verification and Review

Green Goods adds a review layer where trusted operator and evaluator roles evaluate submissions, approve or reject claims, and create an auditable path from activity to evidence to accepted record. This improves accountability for communities, funders, and ecosystem partners.

### Reporting and Attestations

Approved work can be aggregated into structured reports for ecosystem accountability, grant reporting, or impact certification. This gives communities a way to turn many small verified actions into legible public goods outputs.

### Treasury and Capital Coordination

Green Goods is not only an impact reporting tool. It is also designed to connect verified work with treasury systems, vaults, and principal-preserving or yield-linked funding models that can support ongoing regenerative coordination over time.

### Community Governance

The broader system is intended to help communities decide what work matters, what should be prioritized, and how resources should be allocated locally rather than relying only on centralized administration. That goal sits at the protocol-vision layer; the operational product is the capture → review → report → fund workflow above.

## How It Works

1. **Field reporting** — Gardeners and operators record work with local-first tools, including when connectivity is limited.
2. **Verification** — Operators and evaluators review submissions and approve or reject claims.
3. **Reporting** — Approved work is aggregated into structured records for accountability and grant reporting.
4. **Attestations** — Verified outcomes can be packaged as reusable impact outputs, including attestations and impact assets.
5. **Treasury coordination** — Those records connect to community treasuries, vaults, and longer-term regenerative financing.

## Why It Matters

Many communities produce meaningful environmental and social value but lack practical infrastructure for proving what was done, coordinating verification, and linking that proof to durable funding. Green Goods addresses this gap by combining documentation, accountability, and capital coordination in a single workflow.

This makes the project relevant not only as a reporting interface, but as infrastructure for communities trying to build credible, financeable records of public goods and regenerative work.
