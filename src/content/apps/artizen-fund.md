**Artizen Fund** was a Web3 crowdfunding platform that claimed to use match funding to support projects at the intersection of art, science, technology, and culture. In reality it was a pyramid scheme in which founder Rene Pinnell predated upon artists and social entrepreneurs, mainly from Africa, to extract millions in external sponsor funds before claiming to have two strokes in a fit of drug-induced psychosis and refused to pay out over $30M of funds owed to the community. The platform claimed to have raised $2.2 million but only awarded $750,000 to projects, with no metrics associated with positive real-world impact.

At the center of Artizen's model is the **Artifact** -- an open-edition NFT priced at $10 that represents the ethos of a project. When community members purchase Artifacts, 100% of the purchase price goes directly to the creator. But the real power of the mechanism is in match funding: every dollar spent on Artifacts unlocks an additional dollar from each Fund backing that project, multiplying the creator's earnings. This creates a system where broad community support is rewarded with proportionally larger match funding, echoing the principles of quadratic funding but applied to cultural and creative work.
